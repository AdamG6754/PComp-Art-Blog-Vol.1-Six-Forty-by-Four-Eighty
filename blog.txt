Students in the MIT Design Intelligence Lab took the idea of the modern pixel from a point of specific color in a 2D space to a physical interactive object. The "pixels" can be moved, cycle colors through preset color palettes with a tap, blink when someone holds their finger on the sensor, and communicate to each other through human touch to create unique interactive visual designs at art and design exhibitions.

Regardless of distance from each other (assuming both pixels are within reach), the pixels cannot communicate without a person touching both of them. The sole means of communication is not airwaves like Bluetooth, but electrical signals. With no wires, electrical signals for intra-body communication would be difficult, but the human body (mostly saltwater) makes for an excellent conductor for low-voltage electrical signals. When a pixel is held down until it blinks, that is an indicator that the pixel is in "copy" mode and the pixel is sending an electrical signal through your body from its sensor turned emitter, touching another pixel allows the signal from the first pixel to reach the sensor of the second pixel, prompting it to change color.
